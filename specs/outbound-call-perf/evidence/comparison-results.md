# Outbound call comparison

The shipping implementation is the D1–D6 candidate from `b06f10f79257135a94c00ce0a3762fb5c54100da`. All experiment-only repairs preserve those shipping sources. The original baseline is `d101ce385552e29267d6e8ed42f50163582a30ad`. The comparisons used the task's one DigitalOcean `c-4` Droplet with four Intel Xeon Platinum 8280 vCPUs, Ubuntu 24.04.5, and Linux 6.8.0-149. The loopback caller used CPUs 0 and 1 and two Tokio workers. The fixture and runner used CPUs 2 and 3. The locked x86-64 release artifacts used Rust 1.100.0 and the system allocator. Timed samples use caller-process CPU and per-call monotonic latency.

## What the pilots established

The first pilot used 512 measured calls and 128 warm-up calls per window. The four 64-caller before-refresh arms all completed the requested successful work, but opened 25–33 new TLS sockets in their timed windows. They were excluded from paired performance results. The four 64-caller active-refresh arms all completed 512 calls with the old token, one pending replacement, and zero new sockets. Increasing the warm-up population to 1,024 did not repair before-refresh equivalence and also exposed a fixture shutdown overrun. [Initial pilot](raw/pilot.tar.gz) · [Warm-up pilot](raw/pilot-warm-1024.tar.gz)

Overlay `f6c99a10faf0134c18b642e5c9be2c6d8cde12a6` primes the 64-way windows with 96 positively synchronized resource requests outside timed sampling. It keeps the zero-new-TLS requirement and records priming requests separately from measured grants and resource work. Independent bounded review passed after checking request and socket accounting, the release barrier, excess arrivals, fixture shutdown, and the unchanged validity predicates. Observed performance still depends on matched remote trials with this overlay.

The repaired pilot produced two matched 1,024-call pairs in each 64-way phase. Every arm started with 96 idle sockets, completed all offered requests with the old token, and opened zero new TLS sockets. Before-refresh had only the setup grant. Active-refresh had one additional grant positively pending throughout and ended at least 1.59 seconds before the activity cutoff. [Primed pilot](raw/pilot-primed.tar.gz) · [Archive verification](raw/pilot-primed.receipt.json)

The provisional HTTP matrix recorded matched work for all four pairs of empty HEAD, POST, PUT, and PATCH, buffered single-frame, and headers-only empty and single-frame requests. Empty POST, PUT, and PATCH verified the wire framing. Their CPU differences varied in sign across pairs. Buffered multi-frame had one matched pair and three invalid pairs, each with one unexpected TLS open in one arm. Two of four headers-only late-error pairs differed in peer response bytes and do not support paired timing. These preliminary results are preserved alongside the final run. [Provisional HTTP matrix](raw/core-http-provisional.tar.gz) · [Archive verification](raw/core-http-provisional.receipt.json)

## Conditional alternatives

**Hyper Host insertion.** Retain the existing setting. Four order-balanced pairs per cell with 2,048 offered calls matched framing, resource work, and connections on canonical, alternate, IPv6, caller-Host refusal, and forced reconnect. Normal traffic opened no timed TLS connections. The reconnect diagnostic opened one connection on each side. Canonical caller CPU difference was −5.21 µs per completion at the median, ranging from −31.98 to +10.60 µs. IPv6 was −0.23 µs, ranging from −5.08 to +20.69 µs. Forced reconnect was +6.93 µs, ranging from −9.45 to +16.22 µs. Alternate host spelling had a negative CPU difference in all pairs, with a −1.48 µs median and p99 moving in both directions. The ordinary and reconnect cells did not repeat a CPU gain. [Matched Host timing archive](raw/host-timing.tar.gz) · [Archive verification](raw/host-timing.receipt.json)

The separate unstarted-retry diagnostic recorded one injected retryable cancellation per arm on a positively reused connection. Both pairs delivered all 128 calls per arm exactly once, with matching configured Host, origin-form target, and request body length. They opened no new TLS connections. Their instrumented timing is excluded. [Matched unstarted-retry behavior](raw/host-unstarted-retry.tar.gz)

**Retaining a sole HTTP data frame.** Retain bounded copying. Hyper reads return `split_to(...).freeze()` from the shared receive buffer. Returning that `Bytes` can retain the whole backing allocation even for a short slice, while the configured byte limit bounds only visible bytes. The existing accumulator preserves allocation proportional to the visible result. The unconditional variant lacks that property, independently of timing. In the matched one-frame cell it reduced CPU by 0.442 µs per call at the median of four pairs, with mixed p99. In the tiny-behind-large-header cell CPU and p99 rose in all four pairs, with median paired changes of +0.935 and +12.032 µs. The large and multi-frame cells retained two matched pairs each. Their other pairs opened unexpected TLS sockets and were excluded. [Retained backing constraint](../design/comparison.md#single-http-data-frame) · [Variant timing archive](raw/first-frame-timing.tar.gz) · [Multi-frame timing archive](raw/first-frame-multi.tar.gz)

Heaptrack recorded higher peak live heap in all three matched large-response retention pairs. At 1,024 retained responses it rose from 68.42 to 99.82 MB. At 512 retained responses it rose from 48.52 to 66.90 MB and from 45.51 to 66.98 MB. Each pair held the bodies for one second and matched zero new TLS connections. In the 512-response profile, the largest peak allocation stack shifted from the response accumulator's `append_with_capacity_cap` to Hyper's HTTP/1 receive-buffer reservation. The one-frame and tiny profiles, including the 10 ms hold control, are diagnostics because at least one arm in every pair opened an unexpected TLS connection. The tuned tiny profile attributes about 4.20 MB of peak live allocations to the variant's Hyper receive-buffer stack, but its unmatched pair does not establish a quantitative comparison. The source-derived backing constraint remains the deciding evidence for the tiny case. [Long-hold profiles](raw/retention-profile.tar.gz) · [Tuned profiles](raw/retention-profile-tuned.tar.gz) · [Short-hold diagnostics](raw/retention-short.tar.gz) · [Matched profiler launcher](heaptrack-compat.json)

**Shared service-cache reads.** Retain the mutex. At 64 callers before refresh, the shared lock reduced CPU in all four pairs. Arm medians were 59.938 and 44.981 µs per completion, and the median paired difference was −4.175 µs, with a range of −25.698 to −0.416 µs. The corresponding p99 arm medians were 33.890 and 35.054 ms. The median paired p99 difference was +0.959 ms, with both improvement and regression across pairs. At 64 callers during refresh, CPU arm medians were 50.089 and 54.060 µs, with paired differences from −5.023 to +13.386 µs. Single-caller CPU differences had both signs before and during refresh. The before-refresh CPU gain at 64 callers does not establish a benefit across the required populations that justifies the extra lock transition on refresh-due hits.

Each timing arm completed 2,048 old-token calls with equal provider and resource work and zero new timed TLS connections. Each active-refresh arm observed one pending replacement, finished the measurement before cutoff, and observed publication after release with exactly two total service grants. Each demand diagnostic recorded 64 successful resource requests on one replacement grant, one cancellation, one timeout, and the expected unavailability on driver and registration closure. [Shared-lock timing and behavior archive](raw/shared-cache-timing.tar.gz) · [Archive verification](raw/shared-cache-timing.receipt.json)

**Exact authority before origin validation.** Retain the existing origin check. All 36 pairs matched. The exact-authority variant had a median paired CPU change of +5.090 µs on canonical traffic, with a range of −21.200 to +12.600 µs per completion. Alternate, mixed, and IPv6 results also crossed zero. The mixed diagnostic had a −10.051 µs median and one positive pair, while the fixed canonical workload had no established gain. All 72 wire receipts matched. Accepted case variants and bracketed IPv6 dispatched with the same Host and zero timed connection opens. Caller Host, wrong scheme, wrong port, wrong origin, and userinfo were refused before reaching the peer. The public admission probes matched for accepted default-port and empty-port cases. [Exact-authority timing and behavior archive](raw/exact-authority-timing.tar.gz) · [Archive verification](raw/exact-authority-timing.receipt.json)

**Default-port pooling.** Retain the existing pool key based on source and workload. The configured target keeps one URL spelling per host. The transport distinguishes accepted implicit and explicit default-port authorities, but a fixed spelling never splits one target across those two buckets. The alternate-spelling experiment does not establish recurring mixed spelling or an opportunity to avoid connections in the fixed workload. [Accepted pool decision](../design/comparison.md#default-port-pooling)

### HTTP

All 36 final core HTTP pairs matched, with 2,048 successful calls on each side of each pair. The buffered one-frame cell had lower CPU and p99 in every pair. Its arm medians were 42.569 to 37.103 µs CPU per completion and 248.680 to 242.000 µs p99. Median paired differences were −3.033 µs CPU and −3.652 µs p99. The remaining core HTTP cells had both signs for CPU and p99. Empty HEAD omitted Content-Length, and empty POST, PUT, and PATCH each sent exactly `Content-Length: 0` with no Transfer-Encoding. [Standard HTTP matrix](raw/final-http-standard.tar.gz)

The final multi-frame cell used 1,024 warm-up calls per arm to preserve TLS reuse, with four matched pairs and no new connections. Its median paired CPU difference was −0.078 µs and its median paired p99 difference was +0.660 µs, both with positive and negative observations. The headers-only late-error cell used one warm-up call per arm, observed the final status before the late failure, and matched 2,048 new connections per arm. Its median paired differences were −20.858 µs CPU and −5.250 µs p99, also with both signs. [Multi-frame matrix](raw/final-http-multi.tar.gz) · [Headers-only late-error matrix](raw/final-http-headers-late.tar.gz)

The separate buffered late-error diagnostic used four matched pairs of 128 calls per arm with 16 warm-up calls. Both sides consistently recorded all 128 post-header body failures with status 200 and exactly 128 peer late errors per arm. The fixture's deliberate delay dominated p99 at about 102 ms, and paired CPU and p99 differences had both signs. [Buffered late-error matrix](raw/final-http-buffered-late.tar.gz)

### gRPC

All 72 transport pairs matched, with 18 combinations of admitted and full-registry fallback methods, immediate/unary/three-message responses, and absent/filtered/enabled telemetry. Each arm completed all 2,048 offers, verified peer messages and trace propagation, and made no new connections or grants in the measured interval. The admitted unary cell with filtered telemetry had both lower CPU and lower p99 in all four pairs, with median paired differences of −8.696 and −13.881 µs. The other transport CPU ranges crossed zero. The immediate-response and fallback data contain a mix of effects across telemetry configurations. [gRPC transport matrix](raw/final-grpc-transport.tar.gz)

All 72 decoded pairs also matched. The full-registry fallback three-message cell with filtered telemetry had lower CPU and p99 in all four pairs, with median paired differences of −9.982 and −27.198 µs. The admitted three-message cell with enabled telemetry used more CPU in all four pairs, with a median paired difference of +4.999 µs and a range of +1.342 to +14.155 µs. Its p99 change had both signs. The remaining decoded CPU ranges crossed zero. Every transport and decoded arm passed the peer method, status, message, request-byte and trace checks. The harness recorded a 256-method fill for each fallback arm. [gRPC decoded matrix](raw/final-grpc-decoded.tar.gz)

Across credential, HTTP and gRPC archives the final comparison retained 204 matched timing pairs and four matched demand diagnostics. Each artifact received 410,112 timed offers, with 409,600 successes and 512 expected buffered late-error failures. CPU and tail-latency effects vary by workload on this fixture and host.
